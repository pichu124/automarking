# Replying to a marking appeal / reconsideration request

1. **Collect**: the student's request, the feedback and marks they actually
   received (the exported grade document), the submission files, the task
   and rubric (band descriptors), and the cohort's marks for context.
2. **Check clerical errors first.** Convert the issued marks to the scale the
   student quotes (e.g. app /100 -> LMS points; a 90-mark group component is
   x0.9). A student quoting a different figure from the record often copied
   the wrong line - if the record already meets their request, say so up
   front; if the LMS entry is wrong, correct it in their favour.
3. **Verify every claim** in the request against the submission: sections
   cited exist, numbers match, the thing they say they did is really there
   (not only described in the appeal). Classify each claim accurate /
   partly accurate / inaccurate.
4. **Decide per criterion** from the rubric descriptors and how other
   submissions were marked on that criterion - not from descriptive words in
   the feedback. A note that describes what a report contains does not
   denote a band; the band rests on whether each element the rubric names is
   evidenced. Name the specific missing element that sets the band.
5. **Withdraw anything unfair** in the original feedback (e.g. calling a
   within-limit section "too short") and restate the real basis.
6. **Draft the reply** (the student's language of the request, usually
   English): thank them and acknowledge what they concede; explain how marks
   were determined (rubric as a whole + moderation across all submissions for
   consistency); one section per criterion - what the report did (described,
   not rated), what set the band, the decision; summary; the formal review
   pathway as a placeholder; offer a short meeting; one concrete tip for
   future work.
7. **Rules**: never mention AI tools, calibration mechanics, caps, raw scores
   or other students by name; no new praise words; firm, warm, specific;
   every figure quoted must be verified against the submission.
8. Give the professor a checklist of what to verify before sending (LMS
   entries, separately lodged documents such as peer reviews, placeholders).
